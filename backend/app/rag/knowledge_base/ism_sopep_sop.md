# ISM Code & SOPEP Standard Operating Procedures
## Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan & Safe Bunkering Procedures

## Section 1: Pre-Bunkering Safety Checklist (ISGOTT Guidelines)
Prior to commencing fuel transfer or bunkering operations:
1. Communication Agreement: A pre-transfer conference must be held between the vessel's Chief Engineer and the bunker barge master, agreeing on maximum pumping rate, emergency shutdown signals, and tank capacities.
2. Scupper Plugging: All deck scuppers, drain holes, and save-all trays must be physically plugged with tight-fitting plugs or cement seals to contain any accidental fuel overflow on deck.
3. Bunker Line Testing: Bunker manifold connections must be pressure-tested and secured with full bolting on every flange hole.
4. Red Flag / Red Light: The vessel must hoist Flag "B" (Bravo) by day or an all-round red light by night indicating hazardous liquid bunkering operations.

---

## Section 2: SOPEP Emergency Response to Bunker Overflow or Oil Spill
In the event of an oil spill, tank overflow, or hose rupture during bunkering:
1. Immediate Emergency Stop: The Chief Engineer or duty watch officer must immediately activate the Emergency Bunker Shutdown Valve (ESD) and inform the bunker supplier to halt all pumping.
2. Spill Containment: Deploy shipboard sawdust, oil sorbent pads, and containment booms on deck immediately. Prevent oil from escaping overboard into harbor waters.
3. Mandatory Notification:
   - Port Harbor Master / Coastal State Authority (e.g. Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore - MPA, or Kesyahbandaran Pelabuhan).
   - Vessel's Designated Person Ashore (DPA) and P&I Club insurer.
4. Prohibited Action: Never use chemical dispersants or detergents on oil spills without explicit statutory authorization from the Port Authority.
